Beyond Keywords: Understanding Google's Entity Game

Local SEO Unlocked · March 5, 2025 · 19 min listen

This episode explains why the old habit of building a page for every keyword and city combination can now be seen as spammy, and why entity-based SEO is the better path. Using a bakery as the running example, it shows how to find your primary entity, research related entities and synonyms, structure service and location pages, connect them with internal links, and keep your Google Business Profile in shape.

Key takeaways

  • Drop the near-identical keyword plus city pages. They clutter your site, make it harder for Google to crawl, and can be treated as spam after the helpful content update.
  • Put your brand name on every product or service page so Google connects each offering to your business. Optimize those pages for the product name plus the brand, not a city.
  • Use your Google Business Profile primary category as your primary entity, then build your location page around that category and your city in the title, main heading and copy.
  • Research your category on Wikipedia, including its "See also" section, to find related entities and synonyms you can work naturally into your content.
  • Link your location page to your individual product pages, and add a call to action on each product page that links back to the location page.
  • Keep your Google Business Profile accurate and complete, add high quality photos of your business and products, and ask happy customers for reviews.

Questions this episode answers

Do keyword plus city pages still work for local SEO?

Not anymore. Large sets of near-identical pages targeting a keyword and a city can be seen as spammy and make your site harder for Google to crawl. A cleaner, entity-based structure with distinct service and location pages works better.

What is entity-based SEO?

Entity-based SEO means structuring your website around the concepts Google uses to organize information, such as "bakery" or "dentist," instead of repeating exact keywords. It helps Google understand what your business is, what it offers and how it fits into the broader category.

How does Google use Wikipedia to understand a business?

Google often treats Wikipedia as a reference for what a category of business is and what it typically offers, though it is only one of many sources feeding its knowledge graph. Reviewing the Wikipedia page for your category can reveal related entities and synonyms worth using on your site.
