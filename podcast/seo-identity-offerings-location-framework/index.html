Local SEO Unlocked / Episode 8

The Holy Trinity of SEO Unleashed!

Local SEO Unlocked · March 7, 2025 · 16 min listen

This episode breaks SEO into three connected pillars: who you are, what you do and where you do it. It covers building a consistent brand identity online, showcasing products and services with smart keyword research and dedicated pages, and connecting with local customers through a Google Business Profile, citations, reviews and community-focused content.

Key takeaways

  • Keep your name, address and phone number exactly the same on your website, Google Business Profile, directories and social media. Even small differences can drag down local rankings.
  • Use schema markup to tell search engines exactly what your content is, such as reviews for your specific business, and write an About Us page that tells your story and uses your brand name.
  • Do keyword research by thinking like your customer: what problems they face and what words they use, then use keyword research tools to check demand and competition.
  • Give each product or service its own page with an engaging description that highlights benefits, answers objections and works keywords in naturally. Write meta titles and descriptions that earn the click.
  • Build a content strategy of blog posts, guides, FAQs and videos that answer your audience's questions and show your expertise.
  • For local reach, keep your Google Business Profile accurate and complete, build consistent local citations, ask for reviews and publish local content like event posts, area guides and local client case studies.

Questions this episode answers

What are the three pillars of SEO?

The three pillars are who you are, what you do and where you do it. Who you are covers your brand identity and consistency, what you do covers your products, services and content, and where you do it covers local SEO. They work together to give search engines a complete picture of your business.

Can inconsistent business information hurt local rankings?

Yes. Even small differences in your name, address or phone number across listings can hurt local search rankings. Cleaning up and standardizing that information everywhere it appears can help a business start showing up for near me searches.

Should every service have its own page?

Yes. Each product or service should have a dedicated page with a clear, compelling description that naturally includes the keywords customers use. That gives search engines a clear signal and gives visitors a reason to take action.
