SEO Unfiltered: The Truth About Vanity Metrics & What Really Matters

Local SEO Unlocked · March 10, 2025 · 14 min listen

This episode explains why third-party scores like domain authority are vanity metrics that Google does not use for ranking, and why chasing them can pull focus from what matters. It covers attracting the right organic traffic, improving conversions, local SEO, helpful content, technical SEO and user experience, and how to choose KPIs that tie directly to business goals.

Key takeaways

  • Stop chasing third-party scores like domain authority. Google does not use them, and a site with a low score that generates leads beats a high-scoring site that does not convert.
  • Start with keyword research into the exact words your audience types, so you attract visitors who actually want what you offer.
  • Pick KPIs based on your business goal. For leads, track form submissions, email signups, phone calls and conversion rates. For online sales, track organic traffic to product pages, purchase conversion rate and average order value.
  • Set up goals in Google Analytics that match your objectives so you can see how many leads and sales come from organic search.
  • Cover technical SEO basics: fast page speed, a mobile-friendly site, easy navigation and structured data that tells Google what your content is about.
  • Improve user experience with clear headings, visuals that break up text and engaging content, so visitors stay on your site instead of hitting the back button.

Questions this episode answers

Does Google use domain authority to rank websites?

No. Domain authority and similar scores are calculated by third-party tools, not by Google. They can look impressive while the site still gets poor organic traffic, which is why they are considered vanity metrics.

What SEO KPIs should I track instead of vanity metrics?

Track the metrics tied to your business goals. For lead generation that means form submissions, email signups, phone calls and conversion rates, and for ecommerce it means organic traffic to product pages, purchase conversion rate and average order value.

What is dwell time in SEO?

Dwell time is how long people stay on your site after clicking through from a search result. If visitors immediately hit the back button, it signals to Google that your content was not relevant or engaging, so clear headings, visuals and a mobile-friendly design help keep people on the page.
