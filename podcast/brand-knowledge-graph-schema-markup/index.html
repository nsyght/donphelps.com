Schema Shenanigans: Unlocking the Secrets of Brand Visibility

Local SEO Unlocked · March 6, 2025 · 11 min listen

This episode looks at how Google sees a brand through knowledge graphs, with one for the overall brand and a separate one for each business location. It covers why consistent citations matter most at the location level, how schema markup labels the parts of a website so Google understands them, and why connected entities give Google a fuller picture of the business.

Key takeaways

  • Think of Google as keeping one file for your brand and a separate file for each location. Each location's file needs the right information, and it needs to be easy to find.
  • Focus citation work on your individual locations and their Google Business Profiles. Consistent listings on reputable directories, industry platforms and local business associations act as votes of confidence.
  • Audit your website, social profiles and directory listings for missing or inconsistent information, then fill the gaps so every source tells the same story.
  • Make your business name, address, phone number and website identical across every profile and platform. It is simple, often overlooked and builds trust with Google and customers.
  • Use schema markup to label what each part of your site is, such as your menu, location page and customer testimonials.
  • Start with a schema generator tool to build the basic markup, then customize it and connect your entities, like products, people and awards, so Google sees how they relate.

Questions this episode answers

What is a brand knowledge graph?

A brand knowledge graph is how Google organizes everything it knows about your business, pulled from your website, social media, directories and reviews. Businesses with multiple locations effectively have one for the brand and a separate one for each location.

Do citations matter for a brand or for each location?

Citations matter most for each individual location tied to a Google Business Profile. Unless the brand has a dedicated headquarters, building citations for the overall brand is less critical than making sure every location is listed consistently.

What does schema markup do for SEO?

Schema markup labels the content on your website so search engines understand what each element is, such as a menu, a location or a testimonial. When that markup connects related entities, Google gets a complete picture of your brand and can match you with the right searchers.
