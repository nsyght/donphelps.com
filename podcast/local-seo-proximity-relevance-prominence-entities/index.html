How to Dominate Local Search: Key Insights for Your Business

Local SEO Unlocked · March 2, 2025 · 14 min listen

This episode breaks down the three factors that decide who shows up in local search: proximity, relevance and prominence, using party rental businesses as the example. It then moves into entities, explaining how Google connects related concepts instead of matching keywords, and why building a web of topical content and industry connections strengthens a business's authority.

Key takeaways

  • Proximity matters most in urgent, mobile searches like "bounce house rentals near me," and Google uses context, not just raw distance, to decide what is convenient for the searcher.
  • List your specific products and services by name. Say "bounce house rentals" or "waterslide rentals" instead of a vague term like "party supplies" so you match the language people actually search with.
  • Customer reviews feed relevance too. When reviewers mention a specific product, Google takes that as a signal you are a strong match for searches about it.
  • Prominence comes from reviews, backlinks and citations. A mention from an event venue, party planning site or local news outlet carries more weight than a listing on a low-quality directory.
  • Treat proximity, relevance and prominence as a set. Being the closest or having the most keywords is not enough on its own.
  • Build entity strength by creating a cluster of related content, such as product type guides, safety tips and party planning ideas, and by earning mentions from industry directories, party planners and event websites.

Questions this episode answers

What are the three main local SEO ranking factors?

The three factors are proximity, relevance and prominence. Proximity is how close the business is to the searcher, relevance is how well its listed services match the search, and prominence is the trust built through reviews, backlinks and citations. A business needs all three working together to rank well.

What is an entity in SEO?

An entity is a distinct concept Google can recognize, such as a person, place, business, product or idea. Google uses entities to connect topics, businesses and user intent, so it can understand the meaning behind a search instead of only matching exact phrases.

How can a party rental company improve its local search rankings?

Name each rental product specifically on the website and Google Business Profile, earn reviews that mention those products, and secure mentions from event venues, party planning sites and local news. Supporting content like guides and safety tips around core products helps Google see the business as an authority.
