Running SEO Across 8 Industries - What Transfers and What Doesn't

By Don Phelps · October 22, 2026 · 6 min read

On any given day I'm thinking about party rentals, dumpster rentals, car washes, hair salons, bridal shops, limo companies, self-storage, and oil change shops. People ask how that's even possible. The answer is that most of SEO is the same everywhere. The part that isn't is where you win or lose.

What transfers between industries?

The foundation doesn't care what you sell. Every local business needs the same base layer:

  • A Google Business Profile that's complete, accurate, and active
  • Clean technical SEO so Google can crawl and understand the site
  • Service pages and location pages that match how people actually search
  • Schema markup that tells search engines and AI exactly who the business is
  • Reviews, and a steady habit of earning more of them

Build that once as a repeatable system and you can roll it out across hundreds of businesses without reinventing anything.

What doesn't transfer?

The searcher. That's the part most agencies skip.

Someone searching for a bounce house is planning a party weeks out. They're comparing options, looking at photos, and checking availability. Someone searching for a dumpster usually needs it this week and wants a price. Someone searching for a bridal shop is making an emotional decision and might browse for months. Someone looking for an oil change wants the closest open shop right now.

Same city, same Google, totally different intent. If you build the same page for all of them, you'll rank for some and convert almost none.

Seasonality will humble you

Party rentals spike in spring and summer. Storage moves with the moving season. Bridal follows engagement season. If you judge a strategy on one month of data without looking at last year's curve, you'll make bad calls. I've learned to compare year over year first and month over month second.

The real lesson: systems plus judgment

Scale comes from systems. Results come from judgment. The system gets every business to a solid baseline. A good analyst then looks at that specific industry and asks, "What does this customer actually need to see before they call?"

That question is the whole job. The tools, the audits, and the reports are just how you answer it.

Why this matters for AI search

AI tools like ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews answer the intent, not the keyword. A searcher who asks "who can drop off a dumpster tomorrow near me" gets a very different answer than "best bounce house rentals for a 5 year old's party." The businesses that understand their customer's real question are the ones those tools cite. Industry knowledge isn't a nice-to-have anymore. It's the strategy.
