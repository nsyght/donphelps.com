What the Military Taught Me About Running a Marketing Team

By Don Phelps · October 22, 2026 · 5 min read

I served overseas before I ever optimized a web page. I didn't know it at the time, but that experience shaped how I'd lead teams for the rest of my career. Marketing isn't combat, and I'd never pretend it is. But a few lessons carried over perfectly.

Own the outcome, not the excuse

In the military, nobody cares why the mission didn't happen. They care that it didn't. That stuck with me.

In SEO, there's always an excuse ready: an algorithm update, a slow client, a developer who didn't push the fix. Some of those are real. But the question I ask my team, and myself, is always the same. What are we doing about it?

Preparation beats talent

The people who did well weren't always the most gifted. They were the ones who prepared. They knew the plan, knew their role, and had thought through what could go wrong.

That's why I'm a big believer in SOPs, checklists, and planning before execution. It isn't bureaucracy. It's how a team performs the same way on a bad day as it does on a good one.

Clear orders, clear ownership

Vague direction gets vague results. Every task on my teams should answer three questions: what needs to happen, who owns it, and when it's due. If any of those are fuzzy, the task will slip, and that's on the leader, not the person doing the work.

Take care of your people

Good leaders eat last. You learn quickly that the team you take care of is the team that shows up for you when it counts.

For me, that means giving people credit in public and feedback in private. It means pushing back on unrealistic deadlines before they hit my team. And it means making sure the people doing the work have what they need to do it well.

Finish what you start

A half-built page doesn't rank. A content plan that stops after month two doesn't build authority. SEO rewards consistency more than almost anything else, and consistency is just discipline over time.

I'm proud of my service, and I don't use it as a marketing line. But when people ask why I run my teams the way I do, the honest answer is that I learned it a long time ago, a long way from home.
